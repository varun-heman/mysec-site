Company

Most security systems record. Very few notice.

We started mySecurity after watching the same scene play out on dozens of sites: rooms full of screens, hours of footage, and an incident nobody saw until the next morning. The cameras worked. The watching didn't.

What we believe

Detection is worthless without response.

An alert that lands in an app nobody opens is theatre. The value is in what happens next — the gate that closes, the person who is actually called, the escalation that doesn't stall. We build the response path as carefully as the model.

False alarms are the real enemy.

Every unnecessary alert trains someone to ignore the next one. A system that cries wolf twenty times a night has made the site less safe, not more. We measure ourselves on the alerts we correctly suppress as much as the ones we raise.

Surveillance capability demands restraint.

We're building something that can watch people all day. That is a serious thing to own. So: processing stays on site, recognition is opt-in per zone, privacy masking is enforced before storage, and every single view is logged — including ours.

The hardware you own is not the problem.

The industry's default answer is a forklift upgrade. Most sites don't need one. They need the cameras they already paid for to be understood. Rip-and-replace is the last resort, not the pitch.

Guards are colleagues, not the thing being replaced.

Humans are bad at staring at quiet screens and very good at judgement, presence and de-escalation. We automate the first and hand them more of the second.

How we build

Field-first, then software.

Every engineer here has stood in a substation at midnight trying to work out why a gate controller won't answer. That's not a rite of passage — it's the only way to build something that survives real sites.

  • Every feature is trialled on a live customer site before it ships
  • Model changes are replayed against archived footage from real deployments
  • Field engineers sit in the same standups as the ML team
  • We publish our false-positive rates to customers, not just our catch rates
