Platform

A security system that understands its own site.

mySecurity is one platform with four layers: an edge appliance that talks to your hardware, models that turn signals into meaning, a policy engine that decides, and actions that actually happen.

Book a technical walkthrough See the architecture
Architecture

Four layers, one loop.

The loop runs continuously, on site, whether or not anyone is watching and whether or not the internet is up.

1 · Device layer

The mySecurity Edge appliance discovers and drives your hardware: ONVIF/RTSP cameras, gate controllers, OSDP and Wiegand readers, intercoms, alarm panels, Modbus and MQTT sensors.

2 · Perception

Vision models describe each scene — people, vehicles, plates, objects, posture, direction, dwell — and sensor models track temperature, contact, vibration and flow.

3 · Policy

Your rules, schedules, zones and escalation ladders. Written in plain language, versioned, testable against historical footage before you turn them on.

4 · Response

Relays, locks, sirens, gate motors, push notifications, calls, SMS, WhatsApp, PSIM/VMS hand-off and third-party monitoring centres — with an evidence trail behind each one.

Video intelligence

Search your cameras the way you'd ask a guard.

Motion detection tells you something moved. mySecurity tells you a delivery van stopped at bay 3 for eleven minutes and nobody got out.

  • Person, vehicle, plate, package, bag, weapon-shape and animal classes
  • Behaviour: loitering, tailgating, running, falling, crowd build-up, wrong-way
  • State: door left open, gate stuck, camera blinded, light failed
  • Plain-English search across weeks of footage — “white pickup at the north gate on Tuesday night”
  • Face and plate recognition, opt-in per site and per zone

Entry decisions that don't depend on who's on duty.

The most common security failure isn't a break-in — it's a gate held open, a door propped, a badge shared. mySecurity drives the hardware directly, so the policy is the behaviour.

Visitors & guests

Every arrival, accounted for.

From a friend dropping in to a hundred-contractor shutdown — the same flow, scaled to the situation.

01

Invite

The host issues a pass from their phone or the console. It carries a purpose, a window and a set of allowed zones.

02

Arrive

QR at the gate, plate at the barrier or ID at the desk. Documents and inductions are checked before the pass activates.

03

Move

The visitor is tracked against their allowed zones. Wandering into a restricted area notifies the host, not the whole site.

04

Leave

Exit is recorded and the pass dies. Anyone still on site at close of day appears on a short, actionable list.

Assets & environment

Watch the things that fail quietly.

Most losses aren't dramatic. A freezer drifts two degrees over a long weekend. A pump trips at 3am. A pallet leaves on the wrong truck. These are monitoring problems, and they belong in the same system as the cameras.

  • Cold-chain and cleanroom temperature, humidity and door-open duration
  • Generator fuel, UPS state, water tanks, pump rooms and utility cut-offs
  • High-value stock zones with camera-confirmed movement
  • Server rack access, rack door state and environment
  • Drift detection: alerts on the trend, before the threshold

Emergency management

The playbook runs itself.

In a real emergency, nobody reads the SOP folder. mySecurity executes it: in order, in seconds, and with a record of every step.

Fire & smoke

Panel signal plus visual confirmation. Egress doors release, the affected zone sounds, lifts recall, and the muster list starts populating from access records.

Intrusion

Perimeter breach escalates through verify → deter → notify → dispatch, with talk-down over the nearest speaker and clips already attached to the guard's alert.

Medical & duress

Fall detection, panic buttons and duress codes route to the nearest responder with the location, the camera view and the person's emergency contacts.

Escalation ladders

If the guard doesn't acknowledge in 60 seconds, it goes to the supervisor. Then the manager. Then the monitoring centre. No alert dies in someone's pocket.

Drills

Run the whole playbook in test mode. Get a timing report showing where the response actually slowed down, not where you assumed it would.

After-action record

A single exportable timeline: sensor readings, clips, decisions, who was notified, who acknowledged, what was done. Insurer- and auditor-ready.
